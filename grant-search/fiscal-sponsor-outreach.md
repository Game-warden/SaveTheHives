# SaveTheHives — Fiscal Sponsor Research & Outreach Draft

Written 2026-08-23. Answers two questions Ronnie raised after reviewing the
One Hive Foundation FAQ: (1) is NC State itself a viable fiscal sponsor,
alongside the three named candidates, and (2) what do those three actually
charge. Ends with a ready-to-send outreach email.

## Is NC State a viable fiscal sponsor?

**No — not in the sense that matters here.** This isn't a policy guess, it's
how the mechanism works structurally:

Fiscal sponsorship is specifically a 501(c)(3) extending its tax-exempt
status to an **outside, unaffiliated** project or individual — that's the
whole point of Players Philanthropy Fund (PPF), Social Good Fund (SGF), and
Third Sector New England (TSNE): they exist to sponsor people who have no
other institutional home. NC State's equivalent machinery — its Office of
Contracts and Grants, its Research Development Office — administers
*sponsored research* funding, but only for NC State's own faculty/staff as
the Principal Investigator on a grant. Ronnie isn't an NC State employee or
affiliate, so SaveTheHives can't become "a project of NC State" the way it
can become "a project of Social Good Fund."

What NC State *can* do is receive funding for **David Tarpy's** research,
with SaveTheHives data/collaboration named inside his grant. That's Path A
from the earlier strategy memo — real, but structurally different from
fiscal sponsorship: the money and the budget authority sit with Tarpy's
grant, not with a SaveTheHives-controlled account Ronnie administers. It's
not a fourth fiscal-sponsor option to add to the list — it's the same
Path A choice already on the table, just clarified. If the goal is Ronnie
keeping control of a SaveTheHives budget (matching his stated wish to lead
the website/platform-development work), a real fiscal sponsor is still the
right mechanism, not NC State.

## Do fiscal sponsors still charge a fee? What do the three candidates charge?

Yes — fiscal sponsorship is a paid service (it's literally their business
model, funding their own compliance/accounting staff). Confirmed current
rates, pulled directly from each org's own site on 2026-08-23:

**Players Philanthropy Fund (PPF)** — **~6% administrative fee** across the
board, per their own site: "PPF's fiscal sponsorship model keeps that to
just 6%," contrasted against the 20–30% nonprofits typically spend running
their own back office. One caveat on their page: "Government-funded grants
are treated under a separate fee structure" — worth asking about directly
if a USDA/NSF award ever flows through them (not relevant to the One Hive
Foundation LOI, which is private philanthropy). No published budget
minimum found. Model not stated on the page reviewed.

**Social Good Fund (SGF)** — **tiered, published rate schedule**, the most
transparent of the three:
- Model A (comprehensive sponsorship): 8% for budgets under $500K, 7% for
  $500K–$1M, 6% above $1M.
- Model C (pre-approved grant relationship — generally the lighter-touch,
  more independence, less integration model in the field): 6.5% under
  $500K, 5% for $500K–$1M, 8% for international projects.
- Plus a **$29/month** admin fee, refundable once $5,000 is raised in a
  year, and waived outright if you already have $10,000 in funding when
  you apply.
- At SaveTheHives' likely budget size (well under $500K), that's **8%
  under Model A or 6.5% under Model C** — SGF's own site doesn't spell out
  which model fits which kind of project, so that's a direct question for
  their intake process.

**Third Sector New England (TSNE)** — **fee percentage not published**
anywhere on their public site (checked their fiscal-sponsorship page and
FAQ directly — both describe services but not pricing). What *is* public:
TSNE's own site states their fiscal-sponsorship application takes an
**8-step process averaging 2–3 months**, the longest and least transparent
of the three. Given the One Hive Foundation LOI is due **September 15,
2026** — about 3 weeks out — TSNE's timeline alone likely rules it out for
this deadline, independent of its fee. Worth a call for future needs, not
this one.

**Bottom line for the Sept 15 deadline:** lead with **PPF (simplest,
lowest disclosed rate, ~6% flat)** or **Social Good Fund (transparent
tiers, fast-sounding $29/mo intake)**. Deprioritize TSNE for this specific
window on timeline grounds, not merit.

## Draft outreach email

Same email works for either PPF or Social Good Fund with the bracketed
org name swapped in — both are used to exactly this kind of quick,
deadline-driven inquiry from a small volunteer project.

---

**Subject: Fiscal sponsorship inquiry — citizen-science project, Sept 15 grant deadline**

Hi [PPF / Social Good Fund] team,

I run SaveTheHives (savethehives.org), a volunteer-built citizen-science
platform that maps and tracks non-managed (feral) honey bee colonies —
crowdsourced sightings, a PWA for logging and re-confirming colonies over
multiple seasons, and a research partnership with Dr. David Tarpy, NC
State University Scholar Professor & Extension Apiculturist, who advises
on the project's scientific framing.

Right now SaveTheHives operates as a sole proprietorship with no 501(c)(3)
or existing fiscal sponsor. I'm looking into fiscal sponsorship because
the One Hive Foundation's Catalyst Grant program (up to $10,000, LOI due
September 15, 2026) requires either 501(c)(3) status or a fiscal sponsor
to apply, and I'd like to submit an LOI for that deadline if a sponsorship
relationship can realistically be in place in time.

A few quick questions before I apply:

1. Given a small, early-stage project (likely well under $500K/year,
   currently effectively pre-revenue outside of small grants), what's a
   realistic timeline to get a sponsorship relationship active — weeks,
   not months?
2. Which of your sponsorship models [if the org offers more than one] fits
   a project like this, and what's the applicable fee at our size?
3. Does your organization have experience sponsoring citizen-science or
   biodiversity-monitoring projects specifically, or academic-adjacent
   partnerships (we work with a named university researcher as scientific
   advisor, not as PI)?

Happy to share more background — a project overview, our current data/
platform, or a call — whatever's most useful on your end. Given the
September 15 deadline this is somewhat time-sensitive; I'd appreciate
knowing early if the timeline realistically won't work so I can plan
around it either way.

Thanks for your time,
Ronnie Bouchon
SaveTheHives
ronniebouchon@gmail.com
