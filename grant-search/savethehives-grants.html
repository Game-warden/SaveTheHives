Grant Prospects for SaveTheHives

A survey of real, currently-trackable funding opportunities for a volunteer-run citizen-science project mapping non-managed honeybee colonies — sorted by how reachable each one actually is today, not by how well it sounds on paper. Covers conservation/citizen-science philanthropy, the research/food-manufacturing/commercial-beekeeping/government-food-supply funders closer to the industry itself, and — as a separate lens — funding worth surfacing for Dr. David Tarpy, not from him.

🗒️
Where things stand: SaveTheHives is currently a sole proprietorship — no 501(c)(3), no fiscal sponsor. That single fact rules more programs out than mission fit does. Everything below is tagged by whether that status is a blocker, and the closing section lays out what it would take to unblock it.

How the five starting hypotheses actually held up

Citizen science fundersMixed. Most big names (NSF AISL, NatGeo open calls) have moved to narrow RFP models with nothing open right now. The best live find wasn't on the original list at all — the Awesome Foundation.
Pollinator conservation fundersWeaker than expected. Most pollinator money is shaped for acreage-based habitat restoration, not colony monitoring — SaveTheHives doesn't plant anything. One Hive Foundation is the one real exception, and even that's an imperfect fit.
NC State / Tarpy seed fundingReal programs exist (Participatory Sciences Microgrants, Wolfpack Challenge), but both are gated behind NC State affiliation — they become useful once the Cooper introduction lands, not before.
Open data / civic tech infra grantsLargely a dead end for now. Cloudflare's Project Galileo is security protection, not funding; Mozilla's Technology Fund and Shuttleworth's grants are invitation/nomination-only. Nothing here to act on.
Youth STEM / education angleThe NC Beekeepers 4-H grant is real money but structurally requires an NCSBA-chapter/4-H partnership; Captain Planet's small grants are the right size but currently paused.
Scientists, food manufacturers, commercial beekeepers, food-supply agencies (added mid-search)The strongest single new find of the whole search: ABF's Friends of the Bee Fund explicitly welcomes non-scientist beekeepers. Everything else in this world — Project Apis m., USDA AFRI, the National Honey Board — is a research-institution funder that only opens up once an academic PI (Tarpy) is willing to lead the proposal.

If a fiscal sponsor becomes worth pursuing

Two of the strongest opportunities above (One Hive Foundation, and eventually larger programs) are blocked only by legal status, not mission fit. Two paths, roughly in order of effort:

Fiscal sponsor A nonprofit "parent" holds the funds and legal liability for a specific project. Setup typically takes a few weeks to a couple of months; the sponsor usually takes 5–10% of funds passing through as an admin fee. Generalist sponsors worth a look: Players Philanthropy Fund, Social Good Fund, Third Sector New England.
NC State host A narrower, informal alternative: ask whether Tarpy's department or the Citizen Science Campus would host a specific grant as a research collaboration, rather than a commercial fiscal-sponsorship relationship. Only works grant-by-grant, and only once the relationship is further along.
File 501(c)3 Form 1023-EZ, self-filed, ~$275 filing fee for orgs expecting under $50K/year; approval typically takes a few weeks to a few months. The bigger lift, but removes the dependency on a sponsor going forward — worth it once funding needs outgrow what fiscal sponsors and individual-friendly grants can cover.
